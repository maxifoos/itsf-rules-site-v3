# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Scope

This document defines the ITSF Standard Matchplay rules of table soccer, or foosball, covering the matchplay rules for both standard singles and standard doubles events. Matchplay rules for other event types are not described in this document.

These Standard Matchplay Rules define how matches are to be played; they do not cover all aspects of tournament conduct, substance use or the dress code.

## 1.2 Document Structure

Most sections in this document contain a description of the process that players are required to follow, which constitute the prescriptive rules, followed by named rules which describe what players must not do; these are the proscriptive rules.

Proscriptive rules are referred to by a name which appears in bold after the text "Rule:". Each rule specifies either a sanction or an award, or both, to be applied in the event of an infraction. A sanction is a penalty given to the offending team, while an award is a compensation given to the non-offending team. Awards and sanctions are specified in title case, and are summarised in the [Awards and Sanctions](./17-awards-and-sanctions.md) section.

Italicised words or phrases are defined terms, which are listed in the [Definitions](./20-definitions.md) section.

These rules are written primarily for matches played without a referee. Where different provisions apply when a referee is present, they are set out in a subsection titled "Refereed Match".

## 1.3 Match Structure and Terminology

In standard singles and standard doubles a match consists of a preliminary process called the *prelude* followed by one or more games separated by a *pause between games*.

Each game consists of periods of *active play* punctuated by a *pause between points*. Active play consists of *active possessions* when the ball is reachable by a rod, and periods when the ball is moving between the rods.

Active play can be briefly suspended by routine breaks called pauses, and also by *interrupts* which are exceptional breaks in play.

Active possessions and pauses are *time-limited*, which means their maximum duration is limited by the rules.
