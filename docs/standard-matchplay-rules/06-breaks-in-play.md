# 6. Breaks in Play

A *break* is a pause or an interrupt, during which active play is suspended:

- pause
  - between points
  - between games
- interrupt
  - ball state interrupt
    - dead ball
    - ball off table
  - player interrupt
  - disruption

## 6.1 Pauses

A *pause* is a routine break in active play, either between points or between games, during which the players may leave the table and converse freely with other people.

Each pause is *time-limited*, meaning that it must not exceed a specified maximum duration, according to the following table:

**Table: Time management for pauses**

| Pause | Pause begins when | Maximum duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Between points | Goal scored that does not end the game | 5 seconds |
| Between games | Goal scored that ends the game | 90 seconds |

Either team is entitled to the full amount of time, but the pause can be terminated early by the verbal agreement of both teams. When the pause ends the ball must be served without delay.

## 6.2 Interrupts

An *interrupt* is an exceptional break in play; either a dead ball, a ball off table, a player interrupt, or a disruption.

While interrupts are not time-limited, they should be resolved without delay, and the ball served without delay.

## 6.3 Dead Ball

A *dead ball* is a stopped ball in the playing area that is not reachable by any rod.

The players in control of the relevant rods must demonstrate that the ball is unreachable before it can be declared dead.

If the only way to contact the ball would be by flexing the rod, then the ball is unreachable and it must be treated as a dead ball.

A dead ball is served without delay from the specified location:

| Location of Dead ball | Serving rod |
| --- | --- |
| Between the 5-rods | 5-rod of the team that had the last kick-off |
| Behind a 5-rod | 2-rod in the nearest goalie area |

:::danger[Rule: Playing the Ball Dead]
A player must not play the ball dead by using an upward movement of a figure.

**Award:** 5-rod Possession Award
:::

:::danger[Rule: Illegal Flex]
A player must not make contact with an otherwise unreachable ball by flexing a rod.

**Award:** 5-rod Possession Award
:::

## 6.4 Ball Off Table

If the ball leaves the playing area and strikes a foreign object either on the cabinet or outside the table, or comes to rest on the top of the cabinet, then a *ball off table* is declared.

The team which had the last controlled possession is identified and the ball is served by the 2-rod of the other team.

If the ball cannot be retrieved within a reasonable time then it should be abandoned and another ball taken from the ball supply.

In general, if the ball strikes any part of the original table then active play continues uninterrupted, but if the ball strikes a table attachment such as a light, a tablet or a video camera, then ball off table is declared and active play stops.

The ball is off the table if it leaves the playing area and:

- strikes a person or any object outside the table (e.g. the floor, ceiling or wall of the room)
- strikes any attachment that is not an original part of the table
- strikes a foreign object resting on the cabinet
- comes to rest on the top of the cabinet
- flies beyond the perimeter of the cabinet

The ball is not off table and active play continues uninterrupted if it returns to the playing area after:

- striking a goal post or crossbar
- striking any part of a goal assembly
- striking any tally marker
- bouncing or rolling on the top of the cabinet
- entering a serving hole from the playing area
- going airborne directly above the playing area or the cabinet

:::danger[Rule: Illegal Airborne Catch]
A player must not catch or otherwise intentionally interact with a ball that is airborne anywhere above the perimeter of the cabinet.

**Sanction:** Conduct Violation
:::

:::danger[Rule: Illegal Aerial]
A player must not perform a controlled transition that causes the ball to directly overfly an opponent's rod.

**Award:** 5-rod Possession Award
:::

## 6.5 Player Interrupt

A *player interrupt* is an exceptional break in active play initiated by one of the players at the table, for example to:

- Make an infraction claim or a goal claim
- Gain consent to clean the ball or playing area
- Relinquish possession of a precarious ball
- Tighten a loose handle or fix a loose wrap
- Request a medical break
- Request table maintenance
- Request a referee

Where possible players should issue player interrupts when they have controlled possession of the ball. In exceptional circumstances a player who does not have controlled possession of the ball can issue a player interrupt by saying "Stop!", at which point active play is suspended, and the ball should be held by whichever player has or can next gain controlled possession.

It is the player's responsibility to ensure that the player interrupt is made at a time that minimises the impact on the match. A player who interrupts play during a controlled transition is in violation of the [Disturbance](./15-player-conduct.md) rule.

A player who needs to ask a brief question, for example to query the event format or the number of remaining time-outs, should avoid interrupting active play and instead wait for the next natural break in play.

## 6.6 Disruption

A *disruption* is an exceptional interrupt to the match which is not directly caused by anyone at the table, for example:

- A figure or other table component breaking
- A lighting failure
- A foreign object or ball appearing near the table or entering the playing area
- A mobile phone or other device making a noise at the table
- Interference from a spectator
- The tournament director asking play to stop

If a disruption occurs during active play the ball should be held by whichever player has or can next gain controlled possession.

If a disruption occurs which consequentially impacts the outcome of a play, then the disrupted play shall not stand. After the disruption is resolved a Replay is given to the team which last had controlled possession.

## 6.7 Infraction Claim

An *infraction claim* is a claim by a player that an infraction has been committed by the opponent. A player may only call an infraction on their own team if play has already been interrupted, but they must not interrupt active play to do so.

:::danger[Rule: Self-Infraction Interrupt]
A player must not interrupt active play to call an infraction committed by their own team.

**Sanction:** First Reachable Rod Possession Award
:::

Players are expected to resolve infraction claims quickly and effectively themselves, in an amicable manner.

An infraction claim is processed according to the following steps:

- If active play is ongoing, the player making the claim initiates a player interrupt
- The ball is held as soon as possible
- The player states which rule they believe was broken
- The current location of the ball is noted
- The teams together perform a fact check
- The teams together resolve the infraction claim
- The appropriate award and/or sanction is determined and applied
- Active play resumes

<details>
<summary>Refereed Match</summary>

In a refereed match the players must not make player interrupts to make infraction claims. The referee will decide whether a rule infraction has occurred and if so will issue a referee interrupt. Under certain circumstances the referee might delay interrupting active play until the outcome of a transition is clear. The referee's ruling can be based on information obtained from the players, spectators, other referees and from available video recordings.

However, a player can interrupt play to make a referee fact challenge or rule challenge. See [Referee and Escalation](./16-referee-and-escalation.md).

</details>

### 6.7.1 Interrupt or Continue

The player making the infraction claim should perform a player interrupt as soon as possible after the alleged infraction occurs. If instead the non-offending team achieves a controlled possession of the ball and then performs a controlled transition, they are deemed to have exercised their option to Continue play from the current location of the ball, and active play shall continue without interruption.

If play is interrupted then the ball must be held as soon as possible; this is the current location of the ball.

### 6.7.2 Fact Check

The player who made the interrupt speaks first and explains what they believe happened. The other players listen and then in turn respond to either agree or disagree. All players should speak honestly regardless of which team they are on.

The fact check concludes either with agreement or disagreement about the facts.

### 6.7.3 Resolution

If the players agree on the facts then the relevant process should be followed according to the rules.

Any advantage obtained by the offending team resulting from or directly following an infraction shall not stand. For any penalty other than a Warning the non-offending team shall also have the option to Continue play from the current location of the ball.

If the players disagree on the facts then they should seek a compromise which is appropriate to the situation. A typical compromise will be a Replay, i.e. a serve from the rod of last controlled possession.

If the players cannot reach a resolution or there are repeated disagreements then the match should continue under referee control.

## 6.8 Goal Claim

A player who wishes to claim that a goal was scored should issue a player interrupt and initiate the fact check and resolution process similar to that for an infraction.
