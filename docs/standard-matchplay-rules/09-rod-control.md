# 9. Rod Control

In doubles one player on each team controls the goalkeeper rod and the 2-rod and the other player controls the 5-rod and the 3-rod. In standard singles each player controls all four of their rods.

A rod is considered to be *under control* when a player is holding that rod's handle using any part of their arm from the elbow down to the fingers. They can release their handles, e.g. to wipe their hands, to change holds, or to move between rods, but must do so in accordance with the rules.

:::danger[Rule: Illegal Rod Control]
During active play a player must not:

- Control a rod using any part of their body beyond the elbow
- In standard doubles, control a rod not designated for their position

Exception: A brief contact of the handle with another part of the body is allowed.

**Sanction:** Conduct Violation
:::

## 9.1 Rod Interference

*Rod interference* occurs when a player's body or clothing touches an opposing team's rod outside the playing area, during active play.

:::danger[Rule: Rod Interference]
During active play, a player must not interfere with an opposing team's rod.

**Award:**
- If the ball is under controlled possession: Replay
- If the ball was in transition: First Reachable Rod Possession Award

**Sanction:** Conduct Violation if the contact is intentional
:::
