# 15. Player Conduct

Players are expected to behave in a sporting manner throughout the match.

:::danger[Rule: Misconduct]
A player must not behave in an unsporting manner, including but not limited to the following:

- Cheating or attempting to cheat
- Willfully or flagrantly breaking a rule
- Committing frequent infractions
- Performing matchplay actions which are obviously excessive in degree or frequency
- Intentionally deviating from the official event format
- Abusing the table or table component
- Using a device during active play
- Cursing
- Arguing with a referee or other match official
- Making direct or indirect insults about a player or official
- Threatening or being aggressive towards any person

**Sanction:** Conduct Violation
:::

## 15.1 Disturbance

A *disturbance* is an unnecessary or excessive player action performed outside the playing area during active play. A disturbance is always illegal regardless of the location of the ball or whether matchplay was affected.

Examples of disturbances include:

- Waving an arm above the table
- Talking loudly or continually
- Making loud sounds or noises
- Talking or making sounds without having controlled possession of the ball
- Unnecessarily repetitive switching between handles
- A player wearing a necklace or similar item which shakes during play

:::danger[Rule: Disturbance]
A player must not cause a disturbance during active play, that is, an unnecessary or excessive action performed outside the playing area.

**Award:** Next Rod Possession Award

**Sanction:** Conduct Violation
:::

## 15.2 Deception

A *deception* is a legal attempt by a player to cause the opposing team to commit to an unfavourable action.

Examples of deceptions include:

- Twitching or otherwise moving a rod as a fake
- A player raising or lowering their head or shoulder
- A player looking away

Any player action which would be illegal in some context cannot be used as a deception. A legal deception can become an illegal disturbance if it is deployed excessively in either frequency or degree.

## 15.3 Coaching

*Coaching* is defined as occurring when a person who is not a player at the table gives a player in the match information or guidance that could be used to their team's benefit during the match.

Coaching should be private and discreet.

Coaching is allowed during the prelude, between points, between games, and during a medical break.

The exchange of information between doubles partners is not coaching, and is allowed at any time, even during active play if they have controlled possession of the ball.

:::danger[Rule: Illegal Coaching]
A player must not solicit coaching or engage with any attempt to coach during active play, or during an interrupt.

**Sanction:** Conduct Violation
:::

## 15.4 Medical Break

A player may request a medical break at any time outside active play, or during active play by initiating a player interrupt when the ball is stopped. Any dispute regarding a medical break should be escalated to the tournament director.

:::note[Refereed Match]
The referee will decide whether the medical break request is approved, and if so, will communicate the maximum duration of the break to the players, which must not exceed 30 minutes. If the request is declined the referee will instruct the player to continue play. If the player is unable to continue after the medical break has expired, then the Tournament Director shall be consulted in order to determine whether the medical break can be extended or if the team shall forfeit the match.
:::

:::tip[Referee Guidance]
A referee has the discretion to allow a personal break for an exceptional essential non-medical issue, for example an urgent phone call or a tournament management responsibility. An urgent bathroom break can be handled as a medical break.
:::
