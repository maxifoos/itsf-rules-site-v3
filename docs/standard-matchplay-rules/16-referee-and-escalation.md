# 16. Referee and Escalation

## 16.1 Escalation Process

Escalation points are global across all rules and accrue per match, as follows:

- Escalation 1: Warning
- Escalation 2: Continue or 5-rod Possession Award
- Escalation 3 and above: Penalty Shot

## 16.2 Referee Request

In an unrefereed match a player may request a referee during a player interrupt or other break in play.

A referee can be requested in order to:

- Provide a rule interpretation
- Settle a disagreement that the players are unable to resolve
- Continue the match with a referee

:::danger[Rule: Illegal Referee Request]
A player must not request a second referee other than during a pause in play.

**Sanction:** Request denied, Conduct Violation
:::

## 16.3 Referee Authority

The referee's authority begins when they arrive at the table, and must be respected at all times during the match.

Players should be aware of their rights and responsibilities when a match is under referee control.

If a referee is officiating then players are not allowed to interrupt active play to call or discuss infractions. Only the referee can interrupt play to declare an infraction. However, a player can make a formal challenge, as set out below.

In response to a rule infraction that affects active play the referee can always award the non-offending team with a Replay.

Under certain circumstances the referee might delay interrupting play until the outcome of a transition is clear.

A referee's ruling can be based on information obtained from the players, from spectators, from other referees and from any available video recordings.

Players are reminded that human error is part of the game.

## 16.4 Referee Instructions

A referee's instructions must be obeyed during a match.

Players must be familiar with the following announcements which a referee can make during active play:

| Referee call | Reason | Player action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Stop!" | Referee interrupt | The ball must be stopped and held |
| "Time!" | Possession time infraction | The ball must be stopped and held |
| "Reset" | Minor infraction | Active play continues |

Players must be familiar with the following announcements which a referee can make during a break in play:

| Referee call | Reason | Player action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Time!" | The maximum time allowed for the pause has expired | Prepare to play |
| "Play!" | Play must now begin | Serve |

It is illegal for a player to question a referee's judgement; this is treated as a Conduct Violation. However, a player can challenge a referee decision if they believe that a factual error has been made or an incorrect award or sanction applied.

Any challenge must be made before active play continues, or if the match has ended, before the referee has left the table.

## 16.5 Fact Challenge

A player can request a fact challenge if they disagree with the referee about what actually happened on the table, for example whether:

- the ball entered the goal
- the ball touched a playing figure
- the ball was overbounced on the 5-rod

A fact challenge may be resolved using the observations of another referee or, if necessary and available, video evidence. Following the review, the challenge is determined to be either successful or unsuccessful.

:::danger[Rule: Two Unsuccessful Fact Challenges]
A player must not make more than one unsuccessful Fact Challenge in a game.

**Sanction:** Penalty Shot
:::

## 16.6 Rule Challenge

If the facts are agreed but a player believes that the referee has incorrectly applied a sanction, then the player can request a Rule Challenge.

The player must state precisely what ruling is being challenged. The referee can either agree with the player and accept the challenge, or obtain a decision from the head referee or the most competent available referee appointed through the tournament office.

If the Rule Challenge is unsuccessful then the requesting team has committed an infraction, and is not allowed to request further Rule Challenges for the duration of the match.

:::danger[Rule: Unsuccessful Rule Challenge]
A player must not make an unsuccessful Rule Challenge.

**Sanction:** Penalty Shot, no further Rule Challenges allowed in the match
:::

## 16.7 Penalty Cards

A player may inform the offending player that their conduct is prohibited and ask them to stop. If necessary, the player may request a referee to resolve the matter.

:::note[Refereed Match]
If the referee believes that a player has violated a rule for which the sanction is a Conduct Violation then they may apply a penalty card. Depending on the nature of the infraction, the referee may issue one or more verbal warnings prior to deploying a penalty card.

A referee who is called to the table to resolve a dispute may issue a Conduct Violation for conduct that occurred before their arrival if the relevant facts can be established.

| Card | Sanction |
| --- | --- |
| Yellow | Penalty Shot |
| First Red | Forfeit Game |
| Second Red | Forfeit Match |

Players must not question or challenge a referee who deploys a penalty card.
:::
